# Visual Math Storyboard

Plan a visual-first math or science explainer with one memorable picture per idea and clear transitions.

## When to use

You want to make an explainer video or animated lesson, not a wall of narration.

## Workflow

1. Find the question that makes the viewer curious.
2. Choose a visual model that can transform as the idea develops.
3. Storyboard each beat as narration, picture, and viewer insight.
4. End by reconnecting the image to the original question.

## Output

Return a usable result for the user’s actual task. State assumptions, evidence, and uncertainty where they affect the result. Keep the output format suited to the job: working HTML, a storyboard, a decision brief, a draft, code, or a short answer. Do not claim that a result was tested, published, or verified unless that action was completed.

## Starter prompt

> Storyboard a 3–5 minute visual explainer about [topic] for [audience]. Do not imitate any creator’s voice or signature style. Build from one concrete question. For each beat, give: narration, exact on-screen picture or animation, transition, and the idea the viewer should notice. Use visual intuition before notation, then connect the visual to the formal rule. Mark claims needing fact-checking and end with a memorable payoff.

## Quality check

Before delivery, check that the result answers the stated need, follows the requested format, and makes any important limitation visible. Invite the user to adjust the result when taste or judgment belongs to them.
